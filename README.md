Migration, Asylum & Border Security in Spain

A Power BI portfolio project exploring migration trends, irregular arrivals, international protection and population dynamics in Spain and the European Union.






1. Project Overview

This project uses Microsoft Power BI to explore migration-related indicators in Spain and the European Union.

It combines official statistics on irregular arrivals, asylum applications, external migration flows and resident population by nationality. The objective is to demonstrate how data cleaning, analytical calculations and interactive visualisation can support the examination of issues relevant to international relations, border management and migration policy.

The report is descriptive rather than predictive. It identifies patterns and differences in published statistics without assuming that observed changes are necessarily caused by a particular policy or geopolitical event.

2. Research Questions

How did recorded irregular arrivals in Spain change between 2024 and 2025?

How do cumulative 2026 figures compare with the equivalent period in 2025?

Which nationalities account for the largest numbers of recorded international protection applications in Spain?

How does Spain compare with other EU countries in first-time asylum applicants?

How have Spain's external migration balance and foreign-national resident population evolved?

3. Dashboard Structure

Page 1 — Irregular Arrivals to Spain

Reference period: Full-year 2024 versus full-year 2025.

Compares recorded arrivals across selected maritime and land-entry territories, with headline indicators for the national totals and their year-on-year change.



Page 2 — Irregular Arrivals: 2026 Update

Reference period: Cumulative figures for 2026 versus the same period in 2025, using the cut-off date specified in the source report.

Examines recent changes in recorded arrivals by territory. Cumulative figures are compared with equivalent periods rather than with full-year totals.



Page 3 — Asylum & International Protection

Reference period: Calendar year 2025, with 2024 comparisons where available in the source data.

Explores international protection applications in Spain, selected nationalities and the distribution of first-time asylum applicants across EU host countries.



Page 4 — Migration Context: Spain

Reference periods:

Net external migration balance: 2015–2024.

External migration flows: calendar year 2024.

Resident population by nationality: 1 January 2024 versus 1 January 2025.

Provides broader demographic context through migration flows, the net external migration balance and changes in the foreign-national resident population.

The source workbook references official statistical publications and datasets from:

Eurostat: first-time asylum applicants and comparisons across EU host countries.

Please consult the original publications and source notes associated with each dataset for definitions, reference periods, revisions and publication cut-off dates.

6. Methodology & Limitations

The analysis combines several statistical measures that describe different aspects of migration. They are not interchangeable.

Irregular arrivals: recorded arrivals do not necessarily represent unique individuals.

Asylum applications: applications are not equivalent to the number of people granted refugee status or other forms of protection.

Net external migration: calculated as the difference between immigration from abroad and emigration abroad; it is not a measure of irregular arrivals.

Foreign-national population: a resident population measure based on nationality, not the number of people who arrived during a particular year.

Period comparability: cumulative 2026 figures must be compared with equivalent periods in 2025. Provisional data and differences in cut-off dates must be considered.

Statistical methodology: changes in source definitions, collection methods and revisions may affect comparability across periods or datasets.

The report focuses on descriptive analysis. Observed trends are not, by themselves, evidence of causation or proof of the effects of a particular policy.

8. Key Learning Objectives

This project was developed to practise and demonstrate:

Cleaning and preparing data from multiple official sources.

Selecting appropriate data types and handling missing values.

Writing DAX measures for meaningful indicators.

Building comparative charts and KPI cards.

Avoiding double counting when totals and their component categories overlap.

Documenting statistical definitions, reference periods and methodological limitations.

Communicating quantitative findings in a security and international relations context.
